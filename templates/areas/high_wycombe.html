Wi-Fi, CCTV and smart home installation across High Wycombe

Wycombe grew up around the chair trade, and it still shows in the housing. The terraces east and west of the centre were built for furniture workers in the 1800s — solid brick, small rooms, long narrow floor plans. A router in the front room simply does not reach the back bedroom through two brick spine walls, and no amount of mesh kit changes the physics. The fix is usually modest: one or two wired access points, put in the right places.

The estates on the valley slopes are a different problem. Built across the 1930s, 50s and 60s, they spread out horizontally rather than upward, so coverage falls away at the edges of the plot long before it fails inside the house. And out on the ridges — Hazlemere, Tylers Green, Downley, Totteridge — the houses get bigger, the gardens get longer, and there is usually an outbuilding, garage or garden office that also needs to be on the network.
